From Louvain to Leiden: Guaranteeing Well-Connected Communities in Large Networks
Abstract: Community detection in complex networks is widely performed using the Louvain algorithm. However, Louvain has a known flaw: it frequently produces internally disconnected communities due to greedy local aggregation. The Leiden algorithm resolves this by introducing a refinement step between local node moving and community aggregation. Leiden guarantees that all discovered communities are well-connected and converges to a stable partition asymptotically faster than Louvain. In hierarchical graph summarization systems like GraphRAG, Leiden is used to cluster knowledge graphs into multi-scale topical modules.
